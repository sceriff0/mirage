Slide registration: one method-agnostic frame, two interchangeable backends (VALIS & STARE tiled)

1 · pipeline 2 · registration 3 · quality control 4 · lazy reads 5 · coarse alignment 6 · accuracy measures

Registration aligns every moving slide of a patient onto that patient's reference slide. The subworkflow (subworkflows/local/registration.nf) performs all method-independent work — grouping by patient, resolving the reference, passing single-slide patients through, generating QC, writing the checkpoint — then dispatches to one of two adapters that emit an identical channel contract. Underneath, the backends are unrelated algorithms: STARE, the shipped default — a JVM-free, tile-parallel registrar of each moving slide onto the reference — and VALIS, a JVM batch registrar kept as the second mode. Both solve the same problem in the same two steps — a global pose fit, then a local residual field — and panels b and d take those two steps apart for STARE. The nuclear/fiducial channel (channel 0) is what drives the alignment in both.

The frame — what happens regardless of method

A strategy/adapter pattern. Both VALIS_ADAPTER and TILED_ADAPTER take the same patient-grouped input and emit the same seven channel names. Everything to the right of dispatch is method-blind.

The null-object rule. A backend that cannot produce one of these emits Channel.empty() for it — never a missing emit, never an error. That is what lets REGISTER_PATIENT wire both backends with one two-line branch, and why nothing downstream has to know which method ran. A third backend inherits the rule: declare every name, empty the ones your method lacks.
Why intrinsic_tre is not called summary. Both backends estimate a target-registration error from their own registration, in different file formats. The slot used to be named after VALIS's artifact (valis_summary) all the way out to the QC report. The channel vocabulary is now unified; the file formats deliberately are not — normalising them is the reader's job, not the seam's.
b

The two steps every backend performs — and why it must be two

This is the one idea to carry away. Both backends answer the same two questions in the same order, with different machinery. Understand the two questions and both implementations become readable.

question 1 · pose

“Where is this slide, roughly?”

Consecutive sections of the same block are placed on the microscope stage independently: they arrive shifted, rotated, sometimes flipped, by hundreds or thousands of pixels. One transform, applied to the whole slide, removes all of it.

what is estimated
ONE matrix for the whole slide — 3–6 degrees of freedom (translation, rotation, ± scale/shear).
search range
Unbounded. A whole-thumbnail rotation sweep (STARE) or feature matching (VALIS) does not care how far apart the two slides start.
cannot represent
Any deformation that varies across the slide — a fold, a stretched edge, a torn region. A single matrix moves every pixel by the same rule.
failure mode
A weak or ambiguous match → a confidently wrong matrix. STARE therefore gates its anchor on the correlation peak and its ratio to the runner-up and flags an anchor it could not verify; VALIS reports its own match error.
question 2 · deformation

“What is still wrong, locally?”

Sectioning physically deforms tissue: it compresses, folds and stretches, differently in different places. What remains after step 1 is a small, spatially varying residual — typically single-digit to a few tens of pixels, and different at every location.

what is estimated
A displacement field — a (dx, dy) per location, thousands of degrees of freedom.
search range
Local only. Optical flow and phase correlation both find the nearest match, within a capture radius. Beyond it they lock onto the wrong structure — silently.
cannot represent
Gross pose. Handed unregistered slides it produces a smooth, plausible, meaningless field.
failure mode
Step 1's residual exceeding step 2's capture radius. This is the single most important invariant in both backends.

Backend 1 — VALIS (registration_method valis, the second mode)

One REGISTER process per patient runs valis-wsi 1.2.0 at its defaults on bolt3x/mirage-valis:1.0.0, over all of that patient's slides at once. MIRAGE changes one argument: the registration channel is passed by index (the nuclear channel, index 0) instead of being matched by name. How VALIS registers is VALIS's own subject; see its documentation.

VALIS_ADAPTER → REGISTER → bin/register.py VALIS 1.2.0 defaults · JVM / Bio-Formats · 1 task per patient method = valis
What MIRAGE adds around it. The registered slides are written in the reference frame (crop="reference"); the registrar is kept as registrar.pickle so QC can replay it; the task asks for 64 GB, doubling per retry.
When to use it. When the rounds do not share a nuclear stain, which STARE requires.
d

Backend 2 — STARE tiled (default): the same two steps, JVM-free and fanned out

The shipped default, --registration_method tiled (STARE: scalable tile-parallel alignment by robust estimation; stare-registration 1.3.0). No batch graph: every moving slide registers directly and independently to the fixed reference — a star topology. NumPy + SciPy + scikit-image + tifffile throughout: no torch, no JVM, no Bio-Formats, no GPU. The two steps map onto four Nextflow processes — the four stages of manuscript Fig. 2a — and step 2 fans out to one task per tile.

Why the memory asks are constants. Three of these peaks are a function of a parameter — the thumbnail cap, the tile read window (core + 3 × stride on each side), the write-tile — and not of the slide's dimensions; only the number of tasks changes with the slide. That is the whole point of the shape, and the reason the alternative was deleted rather than kept behind a flag. The requests are derived from those parameters with floors of 2 GB (anchor), 4 GB (tile task) and 4 GB (resampling) that apply at the defaults, and scale linearly with task.attempt (manuscript Supplementary Table 1, Supplementary Note 3.2).

The one exception is TILED_SOLVE. It holds one displacement vector per lattice node plus several lattice-sized working arrays, so its memory grows with slide area: 0.19–1.20 GB per round pair on the manuscript cohort, against a fixed 2 GB first-attempt request (raised from 1 GB after cohort-sized solves exceeded it). TILED_COARSE asked 48 GB while its anchor was a learned U-Net matcher; the correlation anchor that replaced it peaks below 0.5 GB. conf/modules.config, withName: 'TILED_COARSE' · 'TILED_SOLVE'
step 1Global pose — an NCC rotation sweep on a shared thumbnail TILED_COARSE · bin/tiled_coarse.py
estimatesOne 3×3 matrix M0 per moving slide, mapping moving pixels → reference pixels. The model is euclidean: rotation + translation only, 3 DOF — right when both sections come off the same microscope at the same magnification. Any rotation or scale the anchor leaves is absorbed by the robust affine of the slide-wide solve (step 2b).
seesBoth slides' nuclear/fiducial plane (reg_tiled_nuclear_index; null = resolved from channel metadata against nuclear_markers), read lazily in row bands and decimated by one shared integer factor chosen so the longer side is ≤ reg_tiled_coarse_max_dim (1024 px by default). Peak memory is a band plus the thumbnail — not the plane.
howEach thumbnail is rescaled to [0,1] over a p1–p99.9 window, blurred with a Gaussian of σ = 1 px and set to zero outside an Otsu tissue mask. At 256 px the moving thumbnail is rotated over 360° in 3° steps and each angle is scored by the peak of the normalised cross-correlation. The two best distinct angles are refined on the full thumbnail over ±3° in 1° steps and then ±0.75° in 0.25° steps, and the translation is placed to sub-pixel precision by tenfold-upsampled cross-correlation. M0 is accepted when its correlation peak is at least 0.3 and at least 1.15× the runner-up's. Otherwise a rigid transform is fitted to keypoint matches by RANSAC and accepted with at least 30 inliers; if neither passes, the candidates are compared on up to eight 1024 px nuclear patches read at full resolution, a candidate confirmed by at least three patches is used, and failing that the best candidate is kept and flagged unverified. The translation is then lifted from thumbnail pixels to full resolution.
thenIt writes the tile plan: cores that partition the reference frame exactly — every pixel belongs to one core, no gaps, no overlaps — each of reg_tiled_tile px (2048), one tile task per core. Every node of the slide-wide 64 px lattice belongs to exactly one core. The row count of this CSV is the fan-out width.
Mfull = diag(f,f,1) · Mds · diag(1/f,1/f,1)   ⇒   rotation unchanged, translation × f   — returning M_ds unchanged under-translates by exactly f
  • Why a rotation sweep and not a single correlation. One cross-correlation recovers translation only. A re-mounted section is also rotated, so the correlation is scored at every angle of a full turn and the best angle wins.
  • Why the sweep runs at 256 px. At that size one pixel spans far more than a nucleus (~276 full-resolution px, ~90 µm, on a 70,000 px slide), so the sweep scores the tissue outline rather than individual nuclei — which is what suits the large rotations of a re-mounted section. The 1024 px refinement then works at ~69 full-resolution px per thumbnail pixel.
  • Why one decimation factor for both slides. The two thumbnails are correlated against each other. Per-slide factors would inject a scale difference that a euclidean M0 cannot honestly represent.
  • What max_dim costs. CPU time, not memory: the whole stage peaked at 0.25 GB with a 512 px thumbnail and 0.43 GB with 1024 px, against a 2 GB request. The stage is NumPy + SciPy + scikit-image only — there is no torch or learned matcher in the STARE image. A 256 px floor on reg_tiled_coarse_max_dim is enforced at launch.
the load-bearing invariant The anchor's residual must stay inside what the tile step can recover. A residual of r thumbnail px becomes r × factor full-res px, and the two correlator passes of step 2a recover about two strides per axis (±128 px at the default stride). Beyond that, each tile's true match lies outside the pixels the tile task read — step 2 then measures a shift to the wrong structure and the slide is mis-registered while the run still exits 0. tiled_coarse.py logs a warning (residual ≥ reg_tiled_halo; an anchor left unverified; on the keypoint fallback, fewer than 10 inliers) but nothing downstream checks it, and nothing fails. Read the COARSE log.
step 2aLocal deformation — a lattice of displacement vectors per tile TILED_REG_TILE · one Nextflow task per tile
estimatesFor its one tile: one displacement vector (dx, dy) per lattice node — a node every reg_tiled_stride px (64), so a 2048 px core holds 32 × 32 = 1024 nodes — each with its peak ratio, plus the median of the tile's vectors, recorded as the tile's local error estimate (tre).
seesTwo windows of size tile + 2·(3·stride) (2432² by default, 5.9 Mpx): the reference's read box straight from disk, and the same box of the moving slide after being rigid-warped through M0. Both are in reference-frame coordinates, so what remains between them is pure residual. Only the moving pixels that box draws from are decoded — never the slide. The read margin is three strides (192 px), not reg_tiled_halo.
howAround each node, a correlation window of 2 × stride (128 px); windows with less than 25% tissue are skipped. A first pass at quarter resolution captures displacements of up to one stride and pre-warps the moving crop. The second pass at full resolution subtracts a σ=3 Gaussian blur from both crops to strip the low-frequency intensity mismatch between two differently-stained cycles, applies a Hann window, cross-correlates in the Fourier domain and locates the peak by a three-point Gaussian fit. The kernel mirrors ASHLAR's.
keepsA vector whose correlation peak is at least 1.2× the next local maximum. The normalised correlation error and the Otsu foreground fraction of each crop are also written, for diagnosis only — they are not used for acceptance.
parallelismThese tasks are fully independent: a 16k² slide at tile=2048 is 8×8 = 64 tasks; a 26k² slide is 13×13 = 169. The gather is sized with each slide's own tile count, so a slide's mesh solve starts as soon as its tiles land — not when the whole cohort's do.
step 2bSolve the mesh — robust affine + DCT-PLS TILED_SOLVE · bin/tiled_solve.py

Lays every tile's displacement vectors onto the slide-wide lattice, once per moving slide. The only gate is a range gate:

keep (dx, dy)  if  finite  and  |(dx, dy)| < max_disp   — reg_tiled_max_disp, null = reg_tiled_halo (256 px); not binding: step 2a measures at most ~128 px

Small residuals are kept as measurements, never set to zero. An affine fitted by Huber-weighted least squares is subtracted first, so that a rotation the anchor left is not penalised as roughness. The remainder is smoothed by robust penalised least squares solved with the discrete cosine transform (DCT-PLS) with bisquare weights, which down-weights a confident but locally wrong vector and fills lattice nodes that have none. The smoothing parameter is selected once per slide by spatial block cross-validation. With fewer than 25 vectors it is selected by generalised cross-validation, with fewer than six only the affine is fitted, with one or two a single median translation, and with none the transform reduces to M0 (mesh: null).

The solved lattice defines a MeshField: a regular grid whose values are interpolated by a cubic B-spline into a single continuous displacement field over the whole slide, held constant beyond the outermost lattice nodes. Continuity is the reason a cell straddling a tile boundary is not torn — there is no per-tile discontinuity to fall into. The same field object is sampled by the image warp and by the reg_qc=2 point warper, so QC measures exactly the transform that shipped.

TILED_SOLVE writes *_manifest.json — {ref_slide, slides: {name: {M0, mesh, out_shape}}}, the reference carried as an identity entry. Self-contained JSON, ~kB, three readers.
*_tre.json — coarse_tre_px, n_inliers, n_tiles, mesh_refined, mean/p50/p90/max of the per-tile rigid-stage error, and the full per-tile list — a spatial heatmap VALIS does not provide.
honest limitationThe fan-out's *_tre.json reports the rigid-stage error only. Measuring the residual after the mesh is applied would need a re-warp, which this reduction deliberately does not do; the field is carried as "note": "post-refinement residual not measured here". For a post-registration number, use reg_qc = 2 (panel f) or the benchmark harness.
applyWarp every channel, streamed tile by tile TILED_STITCH · bin/tiled_stitch.py

The forward map composes the global matrix with the field. The field lives in the reference frame — it is applied after M0, not before:

forward:   v = M0 · x   then   u = v + F(v)     x = moving px, u = reference px

Resampling needs the inverse: for each output pixel u, where in the moving slide did it come from? Because F is small and smooth, that is solved by fixed-point iteration to 0.001 px (at most 50 iterations), then one exact affine inverse:

inverse:   v ← u − F(v)  (until |Δv| < 0.001 px)   then   x = M0−1 · v     with no mesh, exactly x = M0⁻¹u
  • Bilinear, on purpose. A bilinear sample is a convex combination of four neighbours — weights non-negative, summing to 1 — so the result can never leave their [min, max]. A non-negative image stays non-negative. Bicubic or Lanczos overshoot near edges and manufacture negative values, which would corrupt downstream marker quantification. Output is then clipped at 0, rounded half-to-even, and clamped to the source dtype, so quantification sees the same integer range it would have on the native slide.
  • Gigapixel-safe streaming. Output is generated one reg_tiled_out_tile (1024²) tile at a time, straight into an uncompressed tiled OME-BigTIFF writer. For each output tile, source_region inverse-maps its four corners, pads by the mesh's largest displacement plus a bilinear pixel, and reads only that box. Peak = one source crop + one output tile, per channel. Neither the whole moving slide nor the whole output is ever resident.
  • Windowed warp = cropped full warp. out_origin shifts the sample grid rather than re-deriving it, so tiles reassemble seamlessly by construction.
TILED_ADAPTER emits registered — one OME-TIFF per moving slide in the reference frame, plus the reference itself flagged is_passthrough (it defines the frame, so it is never warped and is not republished into registered/)  ·  transform + transform_by_slide — the same manifest, keyed per patient and per slide  ·  intrinsic_tre — *_tre.json  ·  stage_checkpoint — empty: STARE composes nothing destructively, so every stage is reconstructible from the manifest and no snapshot is needed.

◆ what changed — the single-task shape is gone

  • The old single-process tiled-register alternative used to sit behind a reg_tiled_fanout flag. It produced identical pixels, but held both whole slides, an all-channel float32 copy and the full warped output live at once — so its memory scaled with the input and had to be budgeted from file size.
  • Both the flag and the process were removed, not merely defaulted off: shipping an unbounded opt-out behind a boolean is a footgun, and the per-tile fan-out is now the only STARE shape.
  • The single-process reference implementation still exists as bin/utils/tiled_pipeline.register_slide, but only as a test and benchmark oracle — the production path is the four processes above. bin/tiled_register.py no longer exists.
  • Unrelated and often confused: the old distributed-VALIS low-memory path (the old per-tile register and per-tile warp processes, …) was archived on 2026-07-24 under archive/tiled-valis-2026-07-24. registration_method = 'tiled' is a separate, live backend and has nothing to do with it.
e

Head-to-head

Same job, same two steps, opposite engineering trade-offs. STARE, the default, measures displacement tile by tile at full resolution on a JVM-free, bounded-memory, embarrassingly-parallel path, and spends more CPU time doing it; VALIS registers a patient's whole series in one large JVM task on downsampled images. On the manuscript's eight-case cohort STARE was the more accurate of the two at about 40-fold lower peak memory (manuscript Fig. 3, Supplementary Table 2).

Which to pick. Stay on the default, STARE (registration_method = tiled). Across 64 round pairs from eight cases it reached a higher matched-pair nuclear Dice than VALIS (median 0.84 against 0.80; higher in all eight cases) and a lower residual centroid distance (0.63 against 0.96 µm), with its largest registration job peaking about 40-fold below VALIS (0.70 against 31.6 GB per case) at about 2.8-fold more CPU time (3.3 against 1.4 CPU-h); no STARE task needed a GPU or more than 2.4 GB. Its transform is also explicit and inspectable: a JSON manifest and a per-tile heatmap rather than an opaque pickle. Pick VALIS (registration_method = valis) when the rounds do not share a nuclear counterstain — STARE requires one in every round, which is why VALIS is kept as the second mode — and one large task per patient (64 GB, doubling per retry) is affordable.

Registration QC — one ladder, both backends

reg_qc (default 2) sets how hard the alignment is checked. ParamUtils.regQcLevel is the single definition; the legacy skip_registration_qc=true forces level 0. QC consumes whichever transform the backend produced — only the warper is method-specific, and both live behind one process.

GENERATE_REGISTRATION_QC
+ cell-overlap2 · default
Segment each slide on its native image with the run's own segmenter, trace the mask to polygons, then warp those through every registration stage and score how well they land — per stage, per cell.
SEG_QC_SEGMENT → SEG_QC_GEOJSON → WARP_SEG_QC
level 2How the cell-overlap score works
  • Segment the natives, with the run's own segmenter. Cells are segmented on each slide's own pre-registration image, so the polygons are ground truth for that slide and are not themselves a registration product. SEG_QC_SEGMENT is SEGMENT under an alias, so --seg_method selects the backend here exactly as it does for the shipped masks — at the default stardist that segmenter reads the nuclear channel (channel 0) only, while instantseg is channel-invariant. SEG_QC_GEOJSON then traces the label image to polygons and is backend-agnostic by construction.
  • Warp points, not pixels. The polygon vertices are pushed through the registrar once per stage — VALIS's fwd_dxdy, or STARE's MeshField.warp_points. The scorer (bin/warp_seg_qc.py) is method-agnostic and takes --method.
  • Pair cells once, at the rigid stage, then hold it fixed. After rigid, one-to-one pairing within the match radius (seg_qc_pairing, default lsa) is reliable; and because the rigid transform is the one every later stage builds on, the pairing stays valid for all of them. Re-deriving it per stage would let a score move because different cells got paired — not a registration effect.
  • Two metrics per stage: per-pair IoU and centroid residual, reported in px and, where the slide metadata carries a pixel size, in µm. The µm form is the one that reads directly — "1.4 µm after non-rigid" is a sentence a pathologist can act on, and it degrades gracefully where a thresholded IoU falls off a cliff.
  • Deltas are reported against the rigid anchor, so delta_vs_anchor.non_rigid.displacement_px_p50 literally reads as "what the non-rigid stage bought over rigid alone" — and, with the wrong sign, as a warning that a stage made things worse.

No micro stage at the default. At reg_valis_mode default REGISTER's checkpoint records micro_registration=false, so the shipped field is the non_rigid field and a micro stage would duplicate it — WARP_SEG_QC does not emit one. It is passed --micro-reg 0, so rigid is the plain affine. Only the opt-in hires mode runs a micro pass (--micro-reg 2) and adds the stage.
One process, two backends. The old tiled-only warp process was merged into WARP_SEG_QC; container, stages, versionTools and flags now come from lib/WarpBackends.groovy, keyed on the method argument — not on params.registration_method. That is deliberate: resolving VALIS-only flags such as --jvm-heap-gb 8 from the backend table makes it structurally impossible for them to leak onto the JVM-free path.
Why segment on the native slide? Warping cell polygons through the transform and measuring how well they land on the reference's cells converts registration error into the unit biologists actually care about — cell overlap — instead of an abstract pixel shift. It is also the only check that is sensitive to where the error is: a slide can have an excellent mean TRE and a folded corner that ruins every cell in it.

Tuning notes

coarse slow / OOMing?Lower coarse_max_dim (floor 256) — the sweep always runs at 256 px and only the refinement works at this size; its cost is CPU time, not memory (under 0.5 GB measured at 512–2048 px).
residual ≥ halo?The warning means the anchor may lie outside what the tile step recovers (about two strides). Raise coarse_max_dim (costs COARSE time) for a finer anchor; raising halo only widens the range gate and the warning threshold.
mesh too coarse?Lower stride — one vector per stride px; it also halves the displacement a tile can recover.
mesh is null?No displacement vector was accepted, so the transform reduces to the rigid anchor M0.

Schematic 2. Slide registration. (a) The registration subworkflow performs all method-independent work — streaming group-by-patient, reference resolution, single-slide passthrough — then dispatches on registration_method to one of two adapters emitting an identical seven-name channel contract, any slot a backend cannot fill being a Channel.empty() null object; all downstream QC and checkpointing are therefore method-blind. (b) Both backends solve the same two problems in the same order: a global pose fit with few degrees of freedom and unbounded search range, then a local displacement field with many degrees of freedom and a bounded capture radius. The engineering constraint common to both is that step 1's residual must land inside step 2's capture radius. (c) The VALIS backend (registration_method = valis, kept as the second mode because STARE needs a nuclear counterstain shared by every round) registers all of a patient's slides together as one graph with valis-wsi 1.2.0 at its defaults — its own default detector and matcher at 512 px for step 1, a dense optical-flow field at 2048 px for step 2, no micro-registration — changing only the registration channel (index 0, the nuclear channel); reg_valis_mode hires runs VALIS's own high-resolution example instead (micro-rigid, then register_micro at 0.25 × the smallest slide's longest side); it runs on a Bio-Formats JVM with a fixed 10 GB heap and emits a replayable registrar pickle, plus a post-register() checkpoint that lets QC score non_rigid separately from rigid. (d) The default STARE tiled backend (registration_method = tiled; stare-registration 1.3.0; the four stages of manuscript Fig. 2a, parameters in Supplementary Table 1) registers each moving slide independently to the reference with a rigid anchor from a normalised-cross-correlation rotation sweep on a shared thumbnail (256 px sweep, 1024 px refinement; step 1), then displacement vectors measured tile by tile on a 64 px lattice and solved slide-wide by a robust affine plus DCT-PLS into one cubic-B-spline field (step 2), in NumPy/SciPy/scikit-image with no learned matcher, across four Nextflow processes whose tile-task memory is a function of parameters rather than slide size. (e) On the manuscript cohort STARE was more accurate than VALIS at about 40-fold lower peak memory and about 2.8-fold more CPU time; the two backends also differ in parallelism grain and transform inspectability. (f) A shared three-level QC ladder checks alignment; at level 2 native-slide cell polygons are warped through each stage and scored against a correspondence fixed at the rigid anchor, with the two backends' warpers behind one process. (g) Registration defaults. The reg_qc ladder in (f) is one arm of the pipeline-wide QC architecture, which Schematic 3 takes apart in full; Schematic 1 places registration in the whole workflow.
