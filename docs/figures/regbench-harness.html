Regbench harness map

How the registration benchmark turns the cyclic-IF cohort into scores: the inputs it makes for itself, every crossed parameter, where each method's transform comes from, what is scored, what is written, and what the numbers do not say. One dataset, the cohort. The selfcheck and the realcheck are reported beside the numbers and never gate: no run is held back and no row is dropped.

Nothing here has run on the cluster: tested on stand-ins and the launcher's dry run 47 runs · 4 on a GPU · 27 per patient · 6 primary rows

The job graph, end to end

  1. 0

    inputs the benchmark's own

    rb-inputs

    The cohort's samplesheet goes through the pipeline's own preprocessing, --start preprocessing --stop preprocessing, at the shipped defaults except pixel_size, pinned at 0.325 µm. One Nextflow head job (inputs.sbatch); the pipeline submits its own process jobs from it.

    writes csv/preprocessed.csv and the preprocessed slides. manifest.py reads that checkpoint into rounds.csv: one row per moving round, a patient with exactly one reference, one nuclear channel per slide (DAPI or CELLTOX by name).

  2. 1

    nuclei and outlines

    rb-nuclei

    An array, one task per native slide, before any method runs. The pipeline's own segment.py runs StarDist on the nuclear channel, with the flags and params the pipeline renders. Its cell mask (the nuclei grown by 10 px, seg_expand_distance) is traced to outlines by the pipeline's own GeoJSON writer. The bare nuclei are the same run's nuclei mask.

    writes the slide's outline GeoJSON and a trace record, so a case cannot silently be built on outlines from another file.

  3. 2

    prepare the cases

    rb-prepare-multiplex

    One case per (patient, moving round). A round whose outlines are not traced, or were traced from another file than the slide is now, is refused with its reason, never read as an empty set.

    writes case.json and points.npz per case, each with a fingerprint of its inputs (size, modification time, a hash over 16 sampled blocks).

  4. 3

    selfcheck and realcheck reported, never gates

    rb-sc-<run>-64grb-sc-<run>-256grb-sc-<run>-512g

    Does a method's point map follow the image its own writer wrote? The selfcheck draws a known pair with dots, runs the method on it and compares. The realcheck does the same on a real case: the first unit of each VALIS primary run goes ahead with --realcheck, beside the rest of the run. It never gates: no ladder waits for either, no run is kept from starting, no row is left out of a table.

    writes selfcheck.json and realcheck.json; verdict collects them into logs/verdicts.tsv.

  5. 4

    the runs, on memory ladders

    rb-<run>-multiplexrb-<run>-multiplex-retry

    Per run, one task per case (a per-pair method) or per patient (a per-patient method), on the ladders 16, 64, 256, 512 GB at 4 CPUs. A unit climbs only after a memory failure; a retry is submitted after a failure, never queued before one. STARE is several jobs per round: coarse, one job per tile, solve, stitch. Its stage jobs (coarse, tiles, solve) run at the pipeline's per-process allocations, not on the ladders; only its last job (stitch) climbs them.

    writes warped.npz (the moved polygons), run.json (ok, cause, seconds, resources, every departure from the method as shipped) and, for the VALIS primary rows, realcheck.json.

  6. 5

    settle, then score

    rb-settlerb-score-case-multiplexrb-tissuerb-score

    settle gives each result still marked "killed" the cause the scheduler's accounting names. Then an array scores each case once and keeps its record; the tissue job covers the secondary block; the scorer first runs verdict (verdicts.tsv), then score reads the records and writes the tables. Everything waits for every selfcheck and every ladder to end, whatever they said.

    writes tables/*.csv and summary.md.

  7. 6–7

    compute-all after the scorer, alone

    rb-compute-<backend>-<patient>rb-score-compute

    The whole Mirage pipeline, no --start/--stop, once per patient and per backend (valis, tiled), traced by Nextflow. Pinned: seg_method=stardist, pixel_size=0.325, enable_trace; everything else at the shipped defaults and the site profile, so registration runs at the shipped memory_mode='high' unless the site profile says otherwise. The heads run on a few lanes, last and alone, so a cost is not measured under contention the benchmark made itself. Step 7 scores again and adds the cost tables.

    writes pipeline_cost.csv, pipeline_cost_summary.csv, pipeline_cost_by_process.csv. The all row's wall time is a sum over patients and includes queue time.

Job names are the prefixes submit_cohort.sh submits. --score-only repeats step 5 alone; --compute-only submits steps 6 and 7 alone.

How a row is scored

Objects

  • Segmented once per native slide, before any registration
  • Outlines (the headline): StarDist nuclei grown 10 px
  • Bare nuclei: the same numbers in their own _nuclei tables

Pairing

  • Gate 1.5 × the median equivalent radius, per case and per set
  • The pipeline's match_lsa; mutual nearest neighbour above 5,000 cells per component
  • The exact assignment is shown as *_exact and never ranked

Null and reading rules

  • Null: one set shifted four radii, in eight directions
  • Dice and displacement are read only where pair fraction clears the null's maximum
  • The common block: the cases every compared primary row registered

Paired differences

  • Against stare, on the cases both rows have
  • The unit is the patient; its rounds are reduced to their median first
  • Bootstrap interval over patients; "descriptive; no ranking is claimed" at every number of patients

Off-seam rows

  • :non_ashlar and :non_palom repeat every method without the nuclei on those tile or block seams
  • They measure how much of ASHLAR's and PALOM's score rests on an interpolated map

Failures

  • The method's own failure is scored at the unregistered pose, never a gap
  • A loss that is not the method's (scheduler, the benchmark's own memory, identical overlaps) is left out of the summaries and listed
  • Counted as failed (method), failed (benchmark) and not run / lost; every event is a row of failure_log, with whose it is

Cost

  • One definition of an attempt for every method: good, wasted, lost
  • CPU-hours from the cgroup counter, reserved CPU-hours, GB-hours, a scheduler cross-check
  • Each method's clock stops at its own written image

Compute-all secondary

  • Per-task rows from Nextflow's trace: CPU-h used, CPU-h reserved, GB-h reserved
  • A different clock from the methods' registration cost: no total adds the two

Caveats that travel with the numbers

  1. Whose settings. STARE's and the pipeline's VALIS settings were chosen by their authors on this cohort with this metric: stare and valis_mirage are in-sample. The other methods were not tuned on it, but "package defaults" describes only some: valis120_hires is upstream's high-resolution example, deeperhistreg_hr_gpu_asdrawn is a shipped 8192 px set on a GPU, ashlar runs on tiles the benchmark cut from stitched slides with noise added to the empty background, and palom is its documented recipe. This is the largest asymmetry in the comparison.
  2. Two frames. Methods are scored in the frame their own map leads to; the numbers differ slightly between frames.
  3. Per-patient and pairwise methods see different problems and fail in different units, so failures are counted both as launches and as rounds.
  4. Self-consistency, not accuracy. There are no landmarks on real slides.
  5. The ladder. Methods need not finish at the same rung, so they need not have had the same memory request; the tables show each run's rung.
  6. ASHLAR's overlaps. About a fifth to two fifths of its nuclei are scored through an interpolated map in the tile overlaps; it is optimistic for ASHLAR when a rotation is left inside its tiles. The :non_ashlar rows measure how much it matters.
  7. DeeperHistReg's primary row follows its writer, not its registration. The written image sits half a field pixel right and down of the registration, so within_1um can be low with a perfect registration. The estimated-field row beside it shows the registration itself.
  8. The cohort's "nuclei" are StarDist nuclei grown by 10 px. Dice and within_1um have a ceiling that is not registration, the same for every method; the bare-nuclei tables show how much is rim.
  9. No ranking is statistically supported. The paired differences carry a bootstrap interval over patients, coarse with few of them; there is no test and no p-value.
  10. The tissue-level block is secondary. ACCREDIT's score on same-modality fluorescence is outside what it was validated for, and it ranks nothing.
  11. Nothing here has run on the cluster. Whether the cgroup counter path works and whether a site confines cores are unverified.
  12. Compute-all is at the shipped defaults but for the two pins (seg_method=stardist, pixel_size=0.325), and may differ from a site's production settings. The all row's wall time is a sum over patients that includes queue time, and the realtime-based CPU-hours do not; the two clocks are never added.
