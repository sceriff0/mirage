Slide registration: one method-agnostic frame, two interchangeable backends (VALIS & STARE tiled)

S1 · pipeline S2 · registration S3 · quality control S4 · lazy reads S5 · coarse alignment S6 · accuracy measures

Registration aligns every moving slide of a patient onto that patient's reference slide. The subworkflow (subworkflows/local/registration.nf) performs all method-independent work — grouping by patient, resolving the reference, passing single-slide patients through, generating QC, writing the checkpoint — then dispatches to one of two adapters that emit an identical channel contract. Underneath, the backends are unrelated algorithms: a JVM deep-feature batch registrar versus a JVM-free per-slide star. Both solve the same problem in the same two steps — a global pose fit, then a local residual field — and panels b–d take those two steps apart in full. The nuclear/fiducial channel (channel 0) is what drives the alignment in both.

The frame — what happens regardless of method

A strategy/adapter pattern. Both VALIS_ADAPTER and TILED_ADAPTER take the same patient-grouped input and emit the same seven channel names. Everything to the right of dispatch is method-blind.

There is no promotion rule: allow_auto_reference was removed, because two sheets differing only in row order chose two different references and nothing downstream recorded that a choice had been made.

The null-object rule. A backend that cannot produce one of these emits Channel.empty() for it — never a missing emit, never an error. That is what lets REGISTER_PATIENT wire both backends with one two-line branch, and why nothing downstream has to know which method ran. A third backend inherits the rule: declare every name, empty the ones your method lacks.
Why intrinsic_tre is not called summary. Both backends estimate a target-registration error from their own registration, in different file formats. The slot used to be named after VALIS's artifact (valis_summary) all the way out to the QC report. The channel vocabulary is now unified; the file formats deliberately are not — normalising them is the reader's job, not the seam's.
b

The two steps every backend performs — and why it must be two

This is the one idea to carry away. Both backends answer the same two questions in the same order, with different machinery. Understand the two questions and both implementations become readable.

question 1 · pose

“Where is this slide, roughly?”

Consecutive sections of the same block are placed on the microscope stage independently: they arrive shifted, rotated, sometimes flipped, by hundreds or thousands of pixels. One transform, applied to the whole slide, removes all of it.

what is estimated
ONE matrix for the whole slide — 3–6 degrees of freedom (translation, rotation, ± scale/shear).
search range
Unbounded. Descriptor matching does not care how far apart the two slides start; it compares appearance, not position.
cannot represent
Any deformation that varies across the slide — a fold, a stretched edge, a torn region. A single matrix moves every pixel by the same rule.
failure mode
Too few or wrong correspondences → a confidently wrong matrix. Both backends therefore report an inlier count alongside the fit.
question 2 · deformation

“What is still wrong, locally?”

Sectioning physically deforms tissue: it compresses, folds and stretches, differently in different places. What remains after step 1 is a small, spatially varying residual — typically single-digit to a few tens of pixels, and different at every location.

what is estimated
A displacement field — a (dx, dy) per location, thousands of degrees of freedom.
search range
Local only. Optical flow and phase correlation both find the nearest match, within a capture radius. Beyond it they lock onto the wrong structure — silently.
cannot represent
Gross pose. Handed unregistered slides it produces a smooth, plausible, meaningless field.
failure mode
Step 1's residual exceeding step 2's capture radius. This is the single most important invariant in both backends.

c

Backend 1 — VALIS (default): the two steps as a deep-feature batch registrar

One REGISTER process per patient, on the upstream cdgatenbee/valis-wsi:1.0.0 image. VALIS ingests all of a patient's slides at once, so step 1 is solved jointly over the whole series rather than pair by pair. Features are learned (SuperPoint), matching is learned (SuperGlue), and both steps run over a Bio-Formats JVM. The pipeline's entire configuration of VALIS lives in one function, build_registrar_kwargs() in bin/utils/valis_config.py.

VALIS_ADAPTER → REGISTER → bin/register.py batch graph over all slides · SuperPoint + SuperGlue · JVM / Bio-Formats · 1 task per patient default
setupIngest the whole patient group at once registration.Valis(...)

Every slide of the patient — reference included — is hard-linked into one input directory and handed to a single Valis object. The reference is named explicitly via reference_img_f; the pipeline always knows it, so register.py's standalone marker-search fallback never runs here.

align_to_reference Truecrop reference create_masks Trueaffine_optimizer_cls None max_image_dim_px 4000
  • crop='reference' is what makes every output share the reference's raster — the reference defines the frame and is never moved.
  • affine_optimizer_cls=None: VALIS's intensity-based affine refiner needs SimpleITK with Elastix bindings, which the image does not carry. Alignment is therefore purely feature-driven.
  • Files are copied, not symlinked, into preprocessed/ — VALIS loses track of src_f through Nextflow's symlinks.
step 1Global pose — learned features, one graph over the series registrar.register()
estimatesOne matrix slide.M per slide, mapping that slide's pixels into the reference frame.
seesEach slide's channel 0 (the resolved nuclear marker), downscaled so the longest side is max_processed_image_dim_px — 2048 px under the default high preset.
howSuperPointFD detects up to 5000 keypoints per slide and describes each with a learned 256-d descriptor. SuperGlueMatcher — a graph neural network — matches two descriptor sets jointly, reasoning about all candidate pairs at once instead of independent nearest-neighbour lookups. VALIS matches across the whole series, builds a graph whose edges are those match sets, and derives each slide's transform toward the reference along it, so the slides land in one consistent space rather than in N independently-fitted ones.
why learnedConsecutive IF cycles are photometrically unlike each other (different exposures, bleaching, sometimes different stains). Hand-crafted descriptors degrade across that gap; SuperPoint/SuperGlue were trained to survive it. This is VALIS's core accuracy claim and the reason it is the default.
costWorking resolution is the memory knob, and the only thing the three presets change.
step 1bMicro-rigid — re-fit the same matrix at higher resolution MicroRigidRegistrar · micro_reg ≥ 1

Passed as micro_rigid_registrar_cls, so it runs inside register() — before the non-rigid stage, not after it. It re-detects features on higher-resolution crops and sharpens slide.M. It does not add a new kind of transform; it improves the accuracy of the step-1 answer.

consequence for QCAt micro_reg ≥ 1 the QC stage labelled rigid means affine ∘ micro-rigid, not the raw affine. By the time the non-rigid stage runs, slide.M is already final.
step 2Local deformation — a dense optical-flow field OpticalFlowWarper
estimatesA dense displacement field per slide — one (dx, dy) per pixel — held as bk_dxdy (backward, used for resampling) and fwd_dxdy (forward, used to warp points, which is what the reg_qc=2 scorer needs).
seesThe rigidly aligned images, at up to max_non_rigid_registration_dim_px — 2048 px under high, 1024 px under medium, 512 px under low (the same size each tier uses for feature matching). The field is then scaled up to full resolution.
howDense optical flow between the aligned nuclear channels: every pixel is allowed to move independently, regularised toward smoothness. With align_to_reference=True each slide is deformed directly onto the reference, rather than serially through its neighbours — errors therefore do not accumulate along the stack.
gotchaVALIS auto-switches to NonRigidTileRegistrar above an internal ~10 GB estimate. That path returns fwd_dxdy as a pyvips.Image, which the Slide setter silently rejects — leaving fwd_dxdy = None and making rigid and non-rigid errors come out identical. register.py carries an explicit safety net that recomputes it by inverting bk_dxdy and re-runs measure_error(). Passing the tile registrar explicitly is deliberately avoided.
step 2bMicro non-rigid — a second field on a crop, composed in register_micro() · micro_reg = 2 · NOT the default

A separate method call after register() returns. It re-estimates the non-rigid field at higher effective resolution on a crop of floor(min_max_size × 0.125), then adds that residual into each slide's existing field: fwd_dxdy ← fwd_dxdy + micro_residual. This pass does not run at the shipped defaults. reg_micro_reg is 1 of a legal 0 | 1 | 2: 0 is no micro-registration at all, 1 is micro-rigid only (it refines slide.M, and is what ships), and only 2 adds this second field. Everything below therefore describes a stage you must opt into with --reg_micro_reg 2.

why a checkpoint must be taken before itThe composition is destructive — afterwards there is no attribute anywhere in the registrar that still holds the pre-micro field, and register_micro() also overwrites {name}_summary.csv with post-micro numbers. The instant between register() and register_micro() is therefore the only moment at which the non_rigid stage can be told apart from micro. That is exactly what --stage-checkpoint-dir captures at reg_qc ≥ 2, and why REGISTER is the only place it can be captured.
step 3Warp and save every slide, all channels registered_slides/*_registered.ome.tiff

Each slide's full channel stack is resampled through M then the field, and written as OME-TIFF cropped to the reference. The process then hard-fails unless the output count equals the input count — a partial warp is a silent data loss otherwise.

xref  =  D( M · xmov )   — M from steps 1+1b, D the field from steps 2+2b, composed into one

The adapter then re-matches each output file to its slide metadata by OME channel signature — the sorted, lower-cased channel-name set read from a manifest of the written OME-XML — never by filename. Two slides of one patient sharing a channel set is a hard error, because the match would silently be ambiguous.

JVM heap. REGISTER always passes an explicit value: min(reg_jvm_heap_gb ?: 32 + 16·task.attempt, task.memory − 4 GiB). So the heap climbs with the retry ramp — 48 / 64 / 80 GiB — which is the fix for the OOMs seen on merged, high-LZW multi-channel references. The older max(8, min(0.75·sysGB, 3·inputGB + 8)) heuristic survives only as valis_config.init_jvm's fallback for callers that pass nothing, and WARP_SEG_QC pins itself at 8 GB rather than use it.
Disk. The process deletes its working copies and VALIS's deformation_fields/ masks/ overlaps/ rigid_registration/ non_rigid_registration/ processed/ intermediates before exiting — but never the staged inputs, which Nextflow needs intact for a retry.
d

Backend 2 — STARE tiled: the same two steps, JVM-free and fanned out

Selected with --registration_method tiled. No batch graph: every moving slide registers directly and independently to the fixed reference — a star topology, which is where the name comes from. NumPy + scikit-image + tifffile for the geometry and I/O, torch + kornia only for the step 1 matcher: no JVM, no Bio-Formats. The two steps map onto four Nextflow processes, and step 2 fans out to one task per tile.

Why the memory asks are constants. Every one of these peaks is a function of a parameter — the thumbnail cap, the tile+halo window, the write-tile — and not of the slide's dimensions. A 26k × 26k slide and a 4k × 4k slide cost the same per task; only the number of tasks changes. That is the whole point of the shape, and the reason the alternative was deleted rather than kept behind a flag.

Constant does not mean small, and TILED_COARSE is the one to watch. Its coarse anchor is a U-Net (DISK), not a cheap feature detector, so its peak is linear in thumbnail area: GB ≈ 1.1 + 7.3 × Mpx. At the shipped reg_tiled_mode high (reg_tiled_coarse_max_dim 2048, 4.19 Mpx) that is ≈31.7 GB, and the closure applies 1.5× headroom to reach the 48 GB above. The parameter-not-slide property still holds; the magnitude is an order out from the other three. This badge said 8 GB × 2^(n−1) with a ~2 GB peak until 2026-09-01 — wrong in the base, and wrong in the ramp shape, which is linear in task.attempt like every other request in this pipeline. conf/modules.config, withName: 'TILED_COARSE'
step 1Global pose — DISK + LightGlue on a shared thumbnail TILED_COARSE · bin/tiled_coarse.py
estimatesOne 3×3 matrix M0 per moving slide, mapping moving pixels → reference pixels. Default model euclidean: rotation + translation only, 3 DOF. (similarity and affine are available; euclidean is right when both sections come off the same microscope at the same magnification, and refusing the extra DOF is what stops RANSAC fitting a bogus scale to a weak match.)
seesBoth slides' nuclear/fiducial plane (reg_tiled_nuclear_index; null = resolved from channel metadata against nuclear_markers), read lazily in row bands and decimated by one shared integer factor chosen so the longer side is ≤ reg_tiled_coarse_max_dim (2048 px, the shipped high tier). Peak memory is a band plus the thumbnail — not the plane.
howEach thumbnail is normalised to [0,1] over a p1–p99.9 window, because DISK was trained on images in that range, then padded to a multiple of 16 (its U-Net downsamples by that factor and raises on a thumbnail that is not), and passed through DISK's from_pretrained("depth") weights for up to 2048 keypoints. LightGlue("disk") matches the two keypoint sets, and the surviving correspondences are fitted by RANSAC — 1000 trials, 3 px inlier threshold — discarding correspondences that disagree with the consensus. The fit and its residual are then lifted from thumbnail pixels to full resolution.
thenIt writes the tile plan: cores that partition the reference frame exactly — every pixel belongs to one core, no gaps, no overlaps — each paired with a read box = core expanded by halo and clamped to the image. Each core's centre is one control point of the future mesh. The row count of this CSV is the fan-out width.
Mfull = diag(f,f,1) · Mds · diag(1/f,1/f,1)   ⇒   rotation unchanged, translation × f   — returning M_ds unchanged under-translates by exactly f
  • Why feature matching and not phase correlation for this step. Phase correlation recovers translation only. Inter-cycle rotation is real and would survive it; feature matching handles both.
  • Why one decimation factor for both slides. LightGlue matches descriptors across the two thumbnails. Per-slide factors would inject a scale difference that a euclidean M0 cannot honestly represent.
  • Why max_dim is not free to raise, and why it is a harder cliff now. DISK is a U-Net, so its activation memory scales with thumbnail area, not time the way the classical corner detector this replaced did: measured on the pinned stack, 3.03 GB at 512 px, 8.78 GB at 1024 px — GB ≈ 1.1 + 7.3 × Mpx. reg_tiled_coarse_max_dim is therefore the memory knob for this stage, not just the runtime knob it used to be; a naive 4096 px thumbnail would ask ~123 GB. There is no CPU fallback front-end — a torch/kornia import failure raises directly, with no classical detector to fall back to on a deployment node where the pinned stack is unavailable.
the load-bearing invariant coarse_tre must stay well below halo. A residual of r thumbnail px becomes r × factor full-res px. If that exceeds the read halo, each tile's true match lies outside the pixels the tile task read — step 2 then measures a shift to the wrong structure and the slide is mis-registered while the run still exits 0. tiled_coarse.py logs a warning (residual ≥ halo, and separately if fewer than 10 inliers support M0) but nothing downstream checks it, and nothing fails. Read the COARSE log.
step 2aLocal deformation — one phase correlation per tile TILED_REG_TILE · one Nextflow task per tile
estimatesFor its one tile: (dx, dy), the leftover shift at that tile's centre, plus tre = hypot(dx, dy) — the tile's local rigid-stage misalignment.
seesTwo windows of size tile + 2·halo (2560² by default): the reference's read box straight from disk, and the same box of the moving slide after being rigid-warped through M0. Both are in reference-frame coordinates, so what remains between them is pure residual. Only the moving pixels that box draws from are decoded — never the slide.
howWhiten each crop (subtract a σ=3 Gaussian blur) to strip the low-frequency intensity mismatch between two differently-stained cycles; Hann window it to taper the non-periodic tile edges the FFT would otherwise wrap around; then cross-correlate with upsample_factor = 10 for 0.1 px precision. The kernel mirrors ASHLAR's.
deliberatenormalization=None — plain cross-correlation, not phase-normalised. Phase normalisation amplifies exactly the high-frequency noise whitening leaves behind, and destabilises the sub-pixel peak on tiles this small.
parallelismThese tasks are fully independent: a 16k² slide at tile=2048 is 8×8 = 64 tasks; a 26k² slide is 13×13 = 169. The gather is sized with each slide's own tile count, so a slide's mesh solve starts as soon as its tiles land — not when the whole cohort's do.
step 2bAssemble the mesh — with TRE gating TILED_SOLVE · bin/tiled_solve.py

Lays every tile's control point onto the grid at its (ix, iy) index. The gate is the one piece of judgement in this step:

disp[iy][ix] = (dx, dy)  if  tre ≥ gate_tre   else  (0, 0)   — gate_tre = reg_tiled_gate_tre, default 1.0 px

A tile already aligned to better than a pixel contributes nothing and stays purely rigid. This is not a speed optimisation — a sub-pixel "correction" measured on a low-texture tile is mostly noise, and writing it into the mesh would bend the field around nothing. If every tile is gated out the entry collapses to mesh: null and the warp is a clean affine, with no interpolation error introduced at all.

The surviving control points define a MeshField: a regular grid whose values are bilinearly interpolated into a single continuous displacement field over the whole slide, edge-clamped (constant extrapolation) beyond the outermost control points. Continuity is the reason a cell straddling a tile boundary is not torn — there is no per-tile discontinuity to fall into. The same field object is sampled by the image warp and by the reg_qc=2 point warper, so QC measures exactly the transform that shipped.

TILED_SOLVE writes *_manifest.json — {ref_slide, slides: {name: {M0, mesh, out_shape}}}, the reference carried as an identity entry. Self-contained JSON, ~kB, three readers.
*_tre.json — coarse_tre_px, n_inliers, n_tiles, mesh_refined, mean/p50/p90/max of the per-tile rigid TRE, and the full per-tile list — a spatial TRE heatmap VALIS does not provide.
honest limitationThe fan-out's *_tre.json reports the rigid-stage error only. Measuring the residual after the mesh is applied would need a re-warp, which this reduction deliberately does not do; the field is carried as "note": "post-refinement residual not measured here". For a post-registration number, use reg_qc = 2 (panel f) or the benchmark harness.
applyWarp every channel, streamed tile by tile TILED_STITCH · bin/tiled_stitch.py

The forward map composes the global matrix with the field. The field lives in the reference frame — it is applied after M0, not before:

forward:   v = M0 · x   then   u = v + F(v)     x = moving px, u = reference px

Resampling needs the inverse: for each output pixel u, where in the moving slide did it come from? Because F is small and smooth, that is solved by three fixed-point iterations, then one exact affine inverse:

inverse:   v ← u − F(v)  (×3)   then   x = M0−1 · v     with no mesh, exactly x = M0⁻¹u
  • Bilinear, on purpose. A bilinear sample is a convex combination of four neighbours — weights non-negative, summing to 1 — so the result can never leave their [min, max]. A non-negative image stays non-negative. Bicubic or Lanczos overshoot near edges and manufacture negative values, which would corrupt downstream marker quantification. Output is then clipped at 0, rounded, and cast back to the source dtype, so quantification sees the same integer range it would have on the native slide.
  • Gigapixel-safe streaming. Output is generated one reg_tiled_out_tile (1024²) tile at a time, straight into a tiled OME-TIFF writer. For each output tile, source_region inverse-maps its four corners, pads by the mesh's largest displacement plus a bilinear pixel, and reads only that box. Peak = one source crop + one output tile, per channel. Neither the whole moving slide nor the whole output is ever resident.
  • Windowed warp = cropped full warp. out_origin shifts the sample grid rather than re-deriving it, so tiles reassemble seamlessly by construction.
TILED_ADAPTER emits registered — one OME-TIFF per moving slide in the reference frame, plus the reference itself flagged is_passthrough (it defines the frame, so it is never warped and is not republished into registered/)  ·  transform + transform_by_slide — the same manifest, keyed per patient and per slide  ·  intrinsic_tre — *_tre.json  ·  stage_checkpoint — empty: STARE composes nothing destructively, so every stage is reconstructible from the manifest and no snapshot is needed.

◆ what changed — the single-task shape is gone

  • The old single-process tiled-register alternative used to sit behind a reg_tiled_fanout flag. It produced identical pixels, but held both whole slides, an all-channel float32 copy and the full warped output live at once — so its memory scaled with the input and had to be budgeted from file size.
  • Both the flag and the process were removed, not merely defaulted off: shipping an unbounded opt-out behind a boolean is a footgun, and the per-tile fan-out is now the only STARE shape.
  • The single-process reference implementation still exists as bin/utils/tiled_pipeline.register_slide, but only as a test and benchmark oracle — the production path is the four processes above. bin/tiled_register.py no longer exists.
  • Unrelated and often confused: the old distributed-VALIS low-memory path (the old per-tile register and per-tile warp processes, …) was archived on 2026-07-24 under archive/tiled-valis-2026-07-24. registration_method = 'tiled' is a separate, live backend and has nothing to do with it.

Head-to-head

Same job, same two steps, opposite engineering trade-offs. VALIS maximises accuracy and cross-slide consistency at the cost of a heavy JVM footprint and one enormous task; STARE trades some accuracy for a JVM-free, bounded-memory, embarrassingly-parallel path.

Step 1 no longer differs in kind between backends — both are now learned matchers (SuperPoint+SuperGlue vs. DISK+LightGlue). They differ in scope: VALIS registers a patient's whole slide series as one graph, while STARE fits each moving slide to the reference independently (the star topology in the row above).
Which to pick. Default to VALIS when accuracy across a whole series matters and a 300 GB, multi-hour task is affordable — the joint graph and the learned cross-modal matcher are its real advantages. Pick STARE when the cluster cannot give one task that much memory, when there is no JVM, when turnaround matters more than the last fraction of a pixel (tiles run concurrently), or when you want an explicit, inspectable transform: a JSON manifest and a per-tile TRE heatmap beat an opaque pickle for debugging a bad alignment.

level 2How the cell-overlap score works
  • Segment the natives, with the run's own segmenter. Cells are segmented on each slide's own pre-registration image, so the polygons are ground truth for that slide and are not themselves a registration product. SEG_QC_SEGMENT is SEGMENT under an alias, so --seg_method selects the backend here exactly as it does for the shipped masks — at the default instantseg that segmenter is channel-invariant, not DAPI-only. SEG_QC_GEOJSON then traces the label image to polygons and is backend-agnostic by construction.
  • Warp points, not pixels. The polygon vertices are pushed through the registrar once per stage — VALIS's fwd_dxdy, or STARE's MeshField.warp_points. The scorer (bin/warp_seg_qc.py) is method-agnostic and takes --method.
  • Pair cells once, at the rigid stage, then hold it fixed. After rigid, nearest-neighbour pairing is reliable; and because the rigid transform is the one every later stage builds on, the pairing stays valid for all of them. Re-deriving it per stage would let a score move because different cells got paired — not a registration effect.
  • Two metrics per stage: per-pair IoU and centroid residual, reported in px and, where the slide metadata carries a pixel size, in µm. The µm form is the one that reads directly — "1.4 µm after non-rigid" is a sentence a pathologist can act on, and it degrades gracefully where a thresholded IoU falls off a cliff.
  • Deltas are reported against the rigid anchor, so delta_vs_anchor.micro.displacement_px_p50 literally reads as "what the non-rigid and micro stages bought over rigid alone" — and, with the wrong sign, as a warning that a stage made things worse.

One process, two backends. The old tiled-only warp process was merged into WARP_SEG_QC; container, stages, versionTools and flags now come from lib/WarpBackends.groovy, keyed on the method argument — not on params.registration_method. That is deliberate: resolving VALIS-only flags such as --jvm-heap-gb 8 from the backend table makes it structurally impossible for them to leak onto the JVM-free path.
Why segment on the native slide? Warping cell polygons through the transform and measuring how well they land on the reference's cells converts registration error into the unit biologists actually care about — cell overlap — instead of an abstract pixel shift. It is also the only check that is sensitive to where the error is: a slide can have an excellent mean TRE and a folded corner that ruins every cell in it.

Supplementary Figure S2. Slide registration. (a) The registration subworkflow performs all method-independent work — streaming group-by-patient, reference resolution, single-slide passthrough — then dispatches on registration_method to one of two adapters emitting an identical seven-name channel contract, any slot a backend cannot fill being a Channel.empty() null object; all downstream QC and checkpointing are therefore method-blind. (b) Both backends solve the same two problems in the same order: a global pose fit with few degrees of freedom and unbounded search range, then a local displacement field with many degrees of freedom and a bounded capture radius. The engineering constraint common to both is that step 1's residual must land inside step 2's capture radius. (c) The default VALIS backend registers all of a patient's slides together as one graph using learned features (SuperPoint + SuperGlue) for step 1, and a dense optical-flow field plus up to two micro passes for step 2; it runs on a Bio-Formats JVM whose heap climbs with the retry ramp, and emits a replayable registrar pickle. Because the micro pass composes destructively, a pre-micro checkpoint is the only way to score the non_rigid stage separately. (d) The STARE tiled backend registers each moving slide independently to the reference with a DISK + LightGlue + RANSAC euclidean fit on a shared thumbnail (step 1), then one phase-correlation per tile assembled into a TRE-gated bilinear mesh (step 2), in NumPy/scikit-image, with torch + kornia only for the step-1 matcher, across four Nextflow processes whose peak memory is a function of parameters rather than slide size. (e) The two backends trade accuracy and cross-slide consistency against memory footprint, parallelism and transform inspectability. (f) A shared three-level QC ladder checks alignment; at level 2 native-slide cell polygons are warped through each stage and scored against a correspondence fixed at the rigid anchor, with the two backends' warpers behind one process. (g) Registration defaults. The reg_qc ladder in (f) is one arm of the pipeline-wide QC architecture, which Supplementary Figure S3 takes apart in full; Supplementary Figure S1 places registration in the whole workflow.
